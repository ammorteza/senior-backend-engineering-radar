# Contributing

Contributions that improve the Senior Backend Engineering Radar are welcome.

For a proposed blip, include its **name**, **quadrant**, **ring**, why it matters to senior backend engineers, expected depth, and important trade-offs.

Prefer durable concepts and broadly useful technologies that improve architecture, reliability, security, performance, operability or engineering effectiveness. Avoid adding something merely because it is new or fashionable.

Keep `radar.csv` compatible with Thoughtworks BYOR. Allowed quadrants are **Techniques**, **Platforms**, **Tools**, and **Languages & Frameworks**. Allowed rings are **Adopt**, **Trial**, **Assess**, and **Hold**.

A ring change should explain what changed: maturity, production experience, adoption, alternatives, or the expected competency of a senior backend engineer.


## Article teaching standard

Articles are practical learning material for Senior engineers. Explain why behavior occurs and how to make or verify a decision. A definition followed by eleven one-sentence summaries is not sufficient. Avoid a fixed prose pattern or a word target; depth should follow the topic.

Preserve these headings exactly:

1. What it is
2. Why it matters for backend engineers
3. How it works
4. Key concepts
5. Production example
6. Trade-offs
7. Failure modes / pitfalls
8. When to use it
9. When not to use it
10. What a Senior Engineer should know
11. What a Staff Engineer should understand

Explain the mechanism step by step where order matters. Define key terms and connect them to consequences. Work through a realistic, subject-specific example with enough context to understand the decision and how its outcome would be verified. Label hypothetical examples appropriately; do not invent measured production results. Code, commands and calculations should clarify a decision rather than decorate the page.

Discuss the causes of failures and the evidence useful in diagnosis. Explain the alternatives in trade-offs and use/non-use guidance. Senior expectations should include implementation and diagnosis; Staff expectations should cover cross-system decisions, ownership and migration where relevant.

Reread an edited article before accepting it. If substantial paragraphs could be pasted unchanged into an unrelated topic, replace them with the missing subject-specific explanation. Check subtle or evolving claims against authoritative primary sources. Preserve strong material when it already meets this standard.

Build success, minimum length and absence of exact duplicates do not establish teaching quality. Review trackers must distinguish completed, preserved and pending articles honestly.
